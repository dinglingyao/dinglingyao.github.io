About

Reasoning beyond observations.

ELLIS PhD researcher at ISTA and MPI-IS · Visiting researcher at Caltech.

Science asks about worlds we never observed. What would the climate look like without human-caused warming? Answering such questions requires representations that preserve scientific variables and mechanisms that remain meaningful under intervention.

My research connects measurement validity, causal identifiability, and physical mechanisms. Together, they form a path from learning patterns in observations to reasoning about how a system would respond to change.

Research

Measure. Identify. Reason.

Three connected steps toward counterfactual reasoning for science—from trustworthy measurements to unseen worlds.

01

Measure

Are we measuring what matters?

From predictions to scientific measurements

A strong predictor is not automatically a valid scientific instrument. TC-Bench tests whether representations preserve physical distinctions; T-MEX examines whether learned proxies support the causal question we want to ask.

ICML 2026 · NeurIPS 2025

TC-BenchT-MEX
02

Identify

What structure makes recovery possible?

Scientific variables anchored by invariance

I study how invariance, multiple views, and dynamical structure make scientific variables identifiable. These principles connect learned representations to the underlying physical parameters and mechanisms across changing conditions.

ICLR 2025 · NeurIPS 2024

InvarianceDynamics
03

Reason

What if the world had been different?

Beyond observations, with mechanisms

My ongoing work combines pretrained emulators with partial knowledge of physical mechanisms to answer unseen intervention questions. Climate offers a motivating case: how would the system respond if CO₂ had followed a different path?
